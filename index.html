01

Current technical programme · ÉTÈ Kernel

Executable does not necessarily mean permitted.

Independent spatial safety layer

The Kernel sits between an AI system's proposed physical action and the runtime that would execute it. It does not replace perception, planning or control. It evaluates whether a proposed action is permitted under an active spatial safety policy.

InputProposed actiontrajectory · command · motion
ÉTÈSafety decisionpolicy · geometry · state
OutputALLOW / REJECTbefore execution
The architectural objective is independence: safety policy should not be freely rewritten by the system whose action it evaluates.
STATUS · prototype / validation stage · hardware enforcement is a development target, not a claimed current capability

What the Kernel validates

  • Geometric boundaries and safety envelopes
  • Trajectory and motion constraints
  • Proximity and dynamic intrusions
  • Multi-agent spatial interactions
  • Fault conditions and invalid states
  • Determinism, repeatability and decision latency

Architectural progression

  • Logical independence
  • Isolated edge execution
  • Hardware-assisted enforcement
02

Validation pathway

One safety architecture, tested from digital to physical.
STAGE 01

Simulation

Controlled, repeatable robotics environments establish the safety decision model.

  • ROS2 / physics-based environments
  • Boundary, proximity and dynamic intrusion tests
  • AI fault injection
  • Decision correctness and repeatability
STAGE 02

Edge

Candidate embedded architectures are evaluated for isolation, determinism and latency.

  • ARM / TrustZone where supported
  • RISC-V / PMP
  • FPGA safety logic
  • Resource and fail-safe behaviour
STAGE 03

Physical

Validated components move into relevant robotics and operational testbeds.

  • AMRs, drones or other autonomous systems
  • Real-time spatial safety decisions
  • Field failure behaviour
  • Evidence for evolving safety practice

The platform is not predetermined. The objective is to identify the architecture that makes independent spatial safety technically credible and ultimately enforceable.

About ÉTÈ

An independent institute for computational governance.

ÉTÈ Institute (Eternal Terra Ear · 永地耳) works across spatial systems, AI, digital governance and humanitarian infrastructure. The institute began by making territory, telemetry, infrastructure and governance computationally inspectable; its current technical programme extends that work into independent spatial safety for Physical AI.

ÉTÈ is fiscally hosted by Open Source Europe in Brussels and works across European and international research, civic and technology ecosystems.

Constructive verificationTechnical claims should be backed by models or tests that others can inspect.

Contestability by designSystems that affect physical or civic environments should leave room for inspection and challenge.
