01

A world model before a decision.

The workbench treats the world as coupled spatial layers. Observation does not jump directly to an answer: Earth data and signals become terrain; terrain becomes infrastructure; infrastructure acquires policy and safety envelopes; an agent proposes an action inside that world; the independent Kernel tests it.

01 · OBSERVE

Earth

Satellite imagery, remote sensing, change detection, terrain and open-source spatial evidence.

02 · LISTEN

Signal

RF spectrum, interference, latency, routing, telemetry and telecommunications state.

03 · COMPOSE

Space

3D geometry, topology, digital twins, cables, corridors, buildings, orbit and physical relationships.

04 · CONSTRAIN

Governance

05 · PROPOSE

Agent

06 · AUDIT

Kernel

03

The Kernel sits after the world model — not inside the agent.

The Kernel is being developed as an independent hardware/software constraint boundary for embodied and physical AI. It is not the world model, not the policy author and not the robot. It evaluates a proposed physical action against independently maintained spatial, operational and governance constraints.

A · OBSERVE

Evidence field

Remote sensing, open-source spatial evidence, telecommunications state, infrastructure telemetry and relevant legal/policy sources.

B · COMPILE

Constraint geometry

Boundaries, safety envelopes, jurisdiction, topology, operating conditions and rights become machine-checkable spatial relationships.

C · INDEPENDENT KERNEL
ALLOW / REJECT

The agent cannot define its own admissibility. The proposed physical action is evaluated against a separate constraint layer and produces a traceable result.

D · RECORD

Signed decision state

Decision, inputs, constraint version and evidence snapshot remain inspectable for later audit and validation.

04

Earth observation extends the signal world.

Remote sensing is not a separate vertical. It gives the telecom/signal model a physical reference: satellites can reveal infrastructure, terrain change and environmental effects; network measurements reveal connectivity and interference. Together they make a richer spatial evidence field.

EARTH / 001

Remote observation

Optical imagery, SAR and change detection can provide independently sourced spatial observations where ground access is incomplete. Uncertainty remains part of the record.

SIGNAL / 002

Telecommunications terrain

OONI, routing, latency, RF and telemetry can be represented as spatial conditions: outage, fragility, interference and redundancy become properties of infrastructure.

TWIN / 003

Active digital twins

Instead of a static model, the twin becomes an auditable spatial structure: evidence enters, agents propose, constraints execute, decisions leave a record.
